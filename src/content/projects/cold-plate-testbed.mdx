## Purpose

The testbed compares 4 cold plate channel geometries for high-heat-flux electronics on the same copper base, coolant loop, and instrumentation: straight, serpentine, high-density multi-channel, and pin-fin. The channel bodies are interchangeable 3D-printed parts, so each geometry runs on the same base.

## Hardware

| Item | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Base plate | C110 copper, 100 × 150 × 3 mm |
| Channel bodies | 3D-printed ASA, interchangeable |
| Seal | Silicone gasket, metal G1/4 bulkheads |
| Heat source | Copper block with two 24 V cartridge heaters, 100 W combined |
| Loop | Reservoir, PWM pump, Hall-effect flow sensor, cold plate, 120 mm radiator |
| Coolant | 90/10 distilled water and propylene glycol |
| Temperature | Five hand-soldered Type-T thermocouples on MAX31856 boards |
| Flow rate | Hall-effect flow sensor, calibrated in-house by catch-and-weigh |
| Pressure drop | Honeywell differential pressure sensor |
| Logging | Arduino Mega at 1 Hz |

## Baseline test: straight channels

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Channels | 9, each 3 × 5 mm |
| Hydraulic diameter | 3.75 mm |
| Flow rate | ≈ 840 mL/min |
| Reynolds number | ≈ 400 to 420, laminar |
| Heater setpoints | 40, 60, 80, 100 W |
| Heat flux tested | 6.25 W/cm² |

<p class="eq">D<sub>h</sub> = 4A / P = 4(3 × 5) / (2(3 + 5)) = 3.75 mm</p>

Thermal resistance stayed between 0.36 and 0.37 K/W at all four power levels, a spread of 3.3%. At a fixed geometry and flow rate, thermal resistance should not depend on power, so a flat result is a consistency check on the rig and not a performance claim.

## What the analysis caught

<Finding title="A 0.187 K thermocouple offset in the energy balance">
  Log statistics showed a 0.187 K offset between a thermocouple pair. With that offset corrected, the energy balance closes to about 98 to 105%.
</Finding>

<Finding title="My first calibration was circular">
  I rejected my own first calibration attempt because it was circular.
</Finding>

<Finding title="Entry-region heat transfer: Nu ≈ 9, not 4">
  The channel flow is in the thermal entry region. The Hausen correlation gives Nu ≈ 9. A fully developed value of about 4 would have been the wrong model and would have cut the predicted heat transfer coefficient by more than half.
</Finding>

<Finding title="The printed walls do almost nothing as fins">
  Fin efficiency is about 0.07 for ASA against about 0.97 for copper. That result is the justification for the copper base.
</Finding>

<Finding title="Pressure drop is below sensor resolution">
  The measured pressure drop is below the resolution of the differential pressure sensor. I report it that way and do not quote a number.
</Finding>

<Finding title="Equal flow rate is the wrong basis for comparison">
  Comparing geometries at equal flow rate confounds velocity with geometry. The test plan now compares them at matched pumping power.
</Finding>

<Finding title="Uncertainty on thermal resistance: ±2.8% (k = 1)">
  From uncertainty propagation through the thermal resistance calculation.
</Finding>

<Finding title="Leaks were root-caused, then fixed">
  The leaks came through the printed layer lines and at the gasket. Interior epoxy and RTV fixed them.
</Finding>

## Open items

<Callout type="limit" title="Read the results with these limits">
  - Serpentine, high-density, and pin-fin are designed and analytically predicted, but not yet tested.
  - Results are n = 1 per condition.
  - Heater power is a setpoint, not a measured value.
  - The tested heat flux, 6.25 W/cm², is far below real GPU heat flux.
